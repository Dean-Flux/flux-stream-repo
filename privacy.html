Flux Stream Repository

Privacy Policy

Updated November 11, 2026

Flux Stream is a personal, noncommercial Kodi add-on maintained by Dean-Flux for a small family group. The add-on runs on your Kodi device. This policy describes the supported Flux Stream configuration: Trakt, TorBox, TMDB, and the separately installed CocoScrapers module.

What stays on your device

Flux Stream saves settings, connected-account identifiers, authorization tokens, cached API responses, search results, and local playback history and progress in its Kodi add-on profile. Trakt data may include your username, watchlists, lists, watched history, and playback progress. TorBox account information may include your email address. These files are not encrypted by Flux Stream. Access to your device, Kodi profile, or backups can expose their contents.

Sign-in takes place with the account provider in your browser. Flux Stream uses authorization tokens rather than asking for your Trakt or TorBox password. The bundled TMDB key identifies the application; it is not a family member's login or personal account token.

What is sent to services

  • Trakt: When connected and its features are enabled, Flux Stream requests account data for watchlists, lists, watched status, and playback progress. With playback tracking enabled, it sends title identifiers and playback events/progress to your Trakt account. Supported account actions can also update watched history or lists. Periodic background requests keep the enabled features current.
  • TorBox: Flux Stream uses your authorization token to request account/cloud information, check source availability, and obtain playback links. Requests can include torrent hashes, file identifiers, and source links needed for the playback action you choose.
  • TMDB: Catalog browsing, artwork, and search requests include the application key or your optional override, title identifiers or search terms, and language preferences.
  • CocoScrapers and source providers: CocoScrapers is a separate add-on. When you search for playback sources, its enabled providers receive the title information needed for that search, such as title, year, identifiers, season, and episode. Those providers operate independently of Flux Stream.
  • GitHub: Kodi downloads the repository catalog, updates, and artwork from GitHub Pages. GitHub also hosts this project's public documentation and issue tracker.

Network requests reveal ordinary connection information, including your IP address, to their destinations. Each service handles that information under its own terms and privacy practices. Flux Stream does not operate a central account, playback-history, or token-storage server. It does operate the limited diagnostic receiver described below. Flux Stream does not send advertising telemetry, and the maintainer does not sell user data.

Retention, disconnection, and deletion

Cached records have different lifetimes depending on their purpose and may remain until expiration/cleanup or until you clear them. Local history, settings, and credentials can remain in the Kodi profile until you disconnect, reset the relevant data, or remove the add-on profile. Disconnecting clears the add-on's connection state; local caches or playback history may need separate removal. This is not a promise of secure erasure from storage or backups.

To stop Trakt background activity, turn off Use Trakt or disconnect it in Accounts. To withdraw Trakt access independently, revoke Flux Stream from your Trakt connected applications. Manage or revoke TorBox access through TorBox as appropriate. Removing the Flux Stream add-on with its profile data, or deleting its plugin.video.personalstream add-on-data folder while Kodi is closed, removes its local profile. Delete exported backups separately.

Disconnecting Flux Stream does not delete watch history or other information already saved in your Trakt or TorBox account. Manage that data directly with the provider. The maintainer cannot remotely erase files on your Kodi device or data held by those providers.

Logs, backups, and support

Send crash reports is on by default starting in version 0.3.24. You can turn it off in Flux Stream Settings > Maintenance > Privacy and diagnostics, or in step 7 of the setup wizard. Turning it off deletes unsent reports and prevents new sends. It cannot recall a request already in progress or a report already received.

After an unexpected shutdown, Flux Stream attempts delivery when Kodi next opens, after a 15-second startup grace period. A forced close or power loss may also trigger detection; a report is not proof of a software crash. First installs and clean shutdowns do not produce reports. Sessions recorded with reporting off are not reported later.

A report contains only Flux Stream/Kodi versions, platform family (such as Windows or Android), session and detection times, a random report/session ID, selected fixed playback/error event names, error types, and relative Flux Stream code locations (file, line, and function). It does not include raw Kodi logs, account tokens, account names/emails, media titles, stream links, or general filesystem paths. Unknown fields and free-form log messages are rejected by the receiver. Report bodies do not contain your IP address, but the hosting infrastructure processes normal connection information.

Reports are sent over HTTPS to the maintainer's personal Vercel Hobby project at flux-stream-reports.vercel.app and stored as private issues in Dean-Flux/flux-stream-reports on GitHub. These resources are owned personally by Dean-Flux and are not hosted under Lesser Media. Vercel and GitHub process data under their own privacy policies. GitHub access is restricted to the maintainer and any collaborators the maintainer explicitly grants access. Reports remain until deleted by the maintainer; request removal through the contact options below and include the report ID if known.

The add-on attempts at most one delivery per startup, with a six-hour cooldown after successful delivery. One unsent summary may remain locally and retry on a later launch for up to seven days after detection. The receiver also limits report size and applies a best-effort daily inbox cap. No GitHub access credential is included in Kodi packages.

Kodi logs can contain activity, titles, provider errors, and diagnostic details. Settings exports and device backups can contain account information depending on the options used. Review and redact files before sharing them. Support requests are optional; anything you post publicly on GitHub is public. Never post tokens, passwords, or unredacted account data.

For privacy questions or to request removal of information you previously supplied to the maintainer, contact Dean-Flux on GitHub through the available account or project contact options. Use the platform's own controls to edit or remove public posts; the maintainer can only act on information under their control.
